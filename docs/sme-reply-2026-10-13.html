1 · The contradictions

Each of these was written down somewhere as settled. It was not.

D-01 Nursing and per-diem codes: we encoded a global ban she never gave contradictsfixed today
"when I previously said nursing codes and per diem codes should not be included, I was talking about medication only Part D or CMM submissions. I do not want those codes removed from the entire system. For home infusion and medical benefit authorizations, those codes absolutely still matter. They just need to be handled in the correct workflow and not thrown into every medication PA."
what we hadA global prohibition, taken from her 3 Aug message: the system prompt said "NEVER include or calculate nursing codes", a test named test_nursing_codes_never_auto_added enforced it, and both eval cases listed 99601/99602 under forbidden_in_output. The project was actively asserting a rule she did not hold.

The rule is now scoped to the benefit rather than applied everywhere: a SubmissionType of medication-only, medical-benefit or undetermined, and a pure screen_service_codes() that withholds those codes from a Part D / CMM submission with a flag naming where they belong, carries them through on a medical-benefit one, and — when the benefit is not documented — refuses to apply the screen at all rather than assuming a default.

The sharpest part: case-002's ban was the wrong way round. That case's expected one-liner carries S9502 x28 — a per-diem code — which makes it a home-infusion medical-benefit submission, exactly the workflow where she says nursing codes still matter. We had banned them on the one case that was entitled to them. The ban is removed there and kept on case-001, where Part D now justifies it.

What survives from 3 Aug is the half she did not retract: the AI never calculates nursing units, because they vary by service dates, day supply and provider. It only decides whether the specialist's number belongs on the submission in hand.

D-02 No drug table — research the medication when the case arrives contradictsQ-08 raised
"I do not really want to design this around a certain number of medications or build a small drug list into the system… I want the AI to be able to research the medication when the case comes in."
what we hadAssumption A5 and board question Q-02 framed this as a choice between a curated table that grows one SME-confirmed row at a time and a full HCPCS dataset. JCODE_TABLE holds four hand-written anti-infectives.

She rejected the premise rather than picking a side. Named sources: CMS, FDA labeling, DailyMed, PrescriberPoint, manufacturer prescribing information. A5 is overturned and Q-02 is closed without either of its options being built. The four rows are now a stopgap for the eval suite, not the architecture.

This is the first place the project's central rule does not obviously hold. A J-code read off a retrieved page is neither a deterministic calculation nor a constrained judgment call — it is testimony from a third party, which is the one thing the design says never to trust without a gate. Raised as Q-08: source precedence when they disagree, cache versus per-case fetch, and whether a retrieved billing unit may enter a calculation at all before a specialist confirms it.

2 · The extensions

Not wrong, but smaller than what she is asking for.

D-04 CMM blanks are four states, not three extendsQ-07 raised
"It needs to understand the difference between a question that was never triggered, a question that does not apply, and a question that actually needs an answer but the chart does not support it."
what we hadThree states: Answered / NotApplicable / Unsupported.

She splits our NotApplicable in two — a conditional branch the form never reached versus a question it did reach that is not relevant to this patient. The distinction matters because 88 of the 186 measured questions are blank: if those two are collapsed, the fill-rate metric is measuring nothing. It also probably splits across layers, since branch state is structural and belongs with the form model while unsupported-by-chart is a judgment call.

D-05 The verdict has to come before the submission is finished extendsnew block owed
"look at the patient's clinicals and help us determine if the patient meets the payer's criteria before we even get all the way through the submission."

Our pipeline produces its output at the end. She wants a readiness read at the start, so that a case that cannot succeed is stopped before the specialist spends the effort. That is a new output with a different input set — clinicals against payer criteria, without the coding and quantity work having happened yet.

4 · The answer that was not one

The five referrals she describes were not attached, and do not exist in any form we can run against.

Board question Q-01 asked for the original referrals and the judgment notes behind the 21 cases. Her reply describes five of them — Ocrevus, Remicade, Tygacil, Daptomycin, Ertapenem — in prose, with no documents. Confirmed as all there is.

So the extraction layer still has no input to be measured against, and the gap just got wider: the real input is hundreds of mixed pages, which is the hardest version of the problem and the one we have zero samples of. Synthesising referrals from the CMM submissions was always circular; now it is worse than circular, because it would produce a tidy one-pager and report the score as if it came from the hard case.

The cheapest honest unblock is no longer "a few referrals" — it is one. A single real de-identified packet would fix the document shape, the noise level and the retrieval depth, and would turn one eval case from format-only into end-to-end. Twenty-one is a nice-to-have; one is load-bearing. Until then the board carries the EXTRACT row as explicitly unmeasured rather than quietly unmeasured.

What her reply leaves standing

Worth saying plainly, because the list above is all movement: nothing in her answers touches the spine of the design.

  • The five-layer split — extraction, zero-trust validation, deterministic calculation, constrained judgment, specialist review — and the rule that deterministic work stays in code.
  • Human review as the last layer. Her v1 explicitly keeps the specialist submitting by hand, which makes full autonomy a non-goal by her own choice and not just ours.
  • Never guess, flag instead. Reinforced twice: unknown drugs, and unsupported diagnoses.
  • Every calculation rule from Q7 and Q8 — single-dose-vial rounding per dose, independent J-code units, schedule-built dose counts. 48 tests, unchanged by this reply.
