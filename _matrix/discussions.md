- [ ] **Q-01 · The case library arrived without the original referrals or the judgment notes.** Her questionnaire answer describes each case as referral plus clinical records, plus the submission, plus the payer response, plus a short note on any judgment call. What actually arrived is the CMM submission and the outcome letter only. This is load-bearing in two directions: without a referral there is no input to test the extraction layer against, so the whole EXTRACT row has no evidence and `S1-G4` can only be closed on calc, coding, format and safety; and without the judgment notes the few-shot material for layer 4 is the three interview answers rather than 21 worked examples. Most likely they simply were not packaged. Asked 2026-08-25 — see the drafted follow-up. (raised 2026-08-25)

- [ ] **Q-02 · How wide is the drug scope, really?** AGENT.md scopes the tool to home IV infusion, and `pa_calc.py` carries a hand-written J-code table with four anti-infectives. The library contains Leqembi, Kisunla, Gammagard, zoledronic acid, milrinone and Vabomere among others. The two readings lead to different engineering: a curated table that grows one SME-confirmed row at a time, or a real HCPCS dataset with the flagging behaviour as the safety net. The second is more work and introduces a data source nobody has validated; the first does not scale past a few dozen drugs. Not decidable without her — asked 2026-08-25. Until it is settled, `T-drug-table-gap` keeps the flag-never-guess behaviour, which is correct under either answer. (raised 2026-08-25)

- [x] **Q-03 · Is Part B versus Part D benefit routing an AI output or specialist-only?** Three library cases were denied under Part D and were Part B territory, the payer citing the Medicare rule that a drug administered through DME is a Part B drug. AGENT.md says provider-specific extras are manual, which argued for leaving this alone; the Q6 answer names wrong-benefit submission as one of the five denial reasons the tool should catch, which argued for building it. (raised 2026-08-25)
  · RESOLVED 2026-08-25, by Edison's delegation, **as a flag and not a decision** — `T-benefit-routing`, under `S1-G6`. The tool surfaces the risk and cites the rule the payer would cite; the specialist decides the benefit. This sits inside the existing architecture rather than adding a sixth layer, keeps full autonomy a non-goal, and is what Q6 actually asks for — a warning while there is still time to fix it. **Revisit if:** the SME says she wants the benefit selected outright rather than flagged; or the flag proves reliable enough over the pilot that leaving the call to a human is just friction. Either would promote this from a gate check to a judgment layer, which is a different shape of work.
